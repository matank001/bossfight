# Marketing examples

Each model's self-selected best concept per brief, then sample judge rationales.

## Brief: `bakery`

**Claude Fable 5.1 — The Defector Program**  
_Bring us their receipt. We'll show you what bread is supposed to taste like._  
Go straight at the chain. Any weekday morning, bring in a receipt from the chain cafe (any date) and trade it for a free slice of warm sourdough with butter—no purchase needed. Pin every surrendered receipt to a 'Wall of Defectors' in the window. It's cheeky, newsworthy, and physically moves chain customers 50 feet into your shop at the exact hour you need them. Portland's indie-versus-corporate reflex does the amplification for you.  
Plan: $3,500 wild-posting + chalk-stencil sidewalk ads in a 3-block radius of the chain ('36 hours. Not a truck.'); $3,000 free slice + butter cost (est. 4,000 redemptions); $3,000 local PR push (Willamette Week, Portland Mercury, Eater PDX, KGW morning) + 3 Portland food creators; $2,500 Instagram/TikTok paid behind 'Wall of Defectors' time-lapse content; $1,500 window installation + receipt pins; $1,500 legal review + contingency. Total $15,000.  
KPI: Weekday 6–11am transactions +20% by week 12; 3,000+ receipts collected; 45% of receipt-redeemers make a purchase same visit; 3+ earned media placements.  
Claims flagged: 3
  - Your coworking space charges $300 a month.: Unsubstantiated comparative pricing claim under FTC guidelines (16 C.F.R. § 233). Stating a specific competitor price benchmark requires documented substantiation of prevailing market rates in the local Portland trade area.
  - 36 hours. Not a truck.: Actionable comparative claim under Lanham Act § 43(a). Implies an objective factual claim about the competitor's supply chain and production methods (i.e., that the chain's bread is shipped in on trucks rather than baked fresh) without empirical substantiation, creating false advertising and trade disparagement liability.
  - Find it and you win 'Bread for a Year' (one loaf a week). Nobody knows which loaf or which day.: Creates an illegal commercial lottery under federal FTC rules and Oregon law (ORS 167.117). The promotion combines prize ('Bread for a Year'), chance (hidden gold mark), and mandatory consideration (purchasing a loaf) without providing a clear and conspicuous Alternative Method of Entry (AMOE / 'no purchase necessary').

**GPT-6.1 Sol — The Crumb Commute**  
_Your morning deserves better than chain reaction._  
Make the independent bakery the easiest weekday stop. At both locations, create a clearly marked preorder pickup point and a rotating sourdough breakfast bundle using existing menu items. Promote a modest, capped first-order offer to nearby commuters. Each concept is an alternative use of the full $15,000 budget, not part of one combined campaign.  
Plan: 12-week budget: mobile preorder setup and pickup signage $2,500; geographically targeted morning ads $4,000; capped introductory offers $4,500; peak-time pickup staffing $2,500; traffic tracking and reporting $1,500. Total: $15,000.  
KPI: Primary: +20% combined weekday foot traffic from opening–11 a.m. by weeks 10–12 versus a two-week prelaunch baseline, measured with entrance counts at both stores. Supporting: 25% of first-time preorder customers return within 30 days; median pickup wait under 3 minutes.  
Claims flagged: 2
  - Locally raised.: Under FTC truth-in-advertising principles, 'locally raised' can be interpreted by consumers as an objective agricultural or sourcing claim indicating ingredients (e.g., grain, dairy, eggs) are locally farmed or sourced. Without documentation substantiating local ingredient sourcing, this claim risks being deemed unsubstantiated or deceptive.
  - Naturally risen.: Claims using 'natural' or 'naturally' in food marketing face regulatory scrutiny from the FTC and FDA. The advertiser must substantiate that no artificial leaveners, chemical dough conditioners, or synthetic additives are used in the sourdough preparation.

**Gemini 3.1 Pro — The Chain Trade-In**  
_Regret your morning coffee? Trade your chain receipt for the real deal._  
A disruptive, highly shareable trial campaign tapping into Portland's 'shop local' pride. Customers bring in a receipt from the new corporate chain competitor and exchange it for a free coffee and sourdough slice at Crumb & Co.  
Plan: $8,000 heavily geofenced mobile ads (targeting devices entering the chain cafe), $4,000 TikTok/IG Reels production (highlighting 'trade-in' reactions), $3,000 cost of goods for giveaways.  
KPI: 1,500 competitor receipts collected, with a 25% email capture and loyalty program sign-up rate to drive repeat weekday visits.  
Claims flagged: 5
  - guaranteed 48-second express morning checkout line / It takes you 48 seconds to grab.: Unsubstantiated performance guarantee under FTC Act § 5. The campaign explicitly guarantees a 48-second transaction, but internal operational KPIs target an average under 60 seconds (admitting transactions will exceed 48 seconds). Express performance guarantees require strict substantiation and clear disclosure of terms/remedies if the guarantee is unmet.
  - sustainable mug: Deceptive environmental marketing under FTC Green Guides (16 C.F.R. § 260.4). Broad, unqualified claims of environmental benefit like 'sustainable' are deceptive unless substantiated by competent and reliable scientific evidence examining the product's entire life cycle.
  - Free refills all morning.: Violation of FTC Guides Concerning the Use of the Word 'Free' (16 C.F.R. § 251.1). The headline asserts unrestricted free refills all morning, but the offer is subject to material limitations not disclosed in the headline (requires a pastry purchase and terminates at 11 AM). Material terms and conditions must be clearly and conspicuously disclosed alongside the 'Free' claim.

## Brief: `budget_app`

**Claude Fable 5.1 — Penny Wars**  
_Your school is losing to State. Fix it with your spare change._  
Turn saving into a school-vs-school rivalry. Every roundup a student saves counts toward their campus total on a public leaderboard across 40 target universities; the winning school's Penny users split a $25k 'Tuition Jar' at the end of 60 days. Built-in referral loop: your school only wins if you recruit your friends, and student orgs (Greek life, clubs, athletic teams) compete as sub-teams.  
Plan: $25k prize pool (the campaign's real media), $20k campus ambassador program (80 paid ambassadors at ~$250 each, 40 schools), $15k TikTok/Instagram geo-targeted paid to the 40 campuses with rivalry creatives, $10k 20 micro-creators at target schools (student-athletes, campus meme accounts), $5k PR push to college press, Barstool-style accounts, and local news, $5k leaderboard web build and in-app team features.  
KPI: Primary: 100k installs with 55%+ from referral/organic (K-factor >= 0.6). Secondary: 60k funded accounts, 5% premium conversion driven by a 'double your team points with Premium' mechanic. Guardrail: blended CPI under $0.80.  
Claims flagged: 5
  - double your team points with Premium: Conditioning increased odds or scoring advantages in a $25k cash prize competition on a paid subscription ($4.99/mo) introduces consideration, constituting an illegal lottery under federal and state sweepstakes laws without an equal, free alternative method of entry (AMOE).
  - Your school is losing to State. Fix it with your spare change.: Presents an unqualified factual claim of leaderboard standing that is unsubstantiated and deceptive under FTC Act Section 5 unless dynamically populated with verified, real-time data for each specific user and campus.
  - shows exactly how much Penny's roundups would have quietly saved them from that same spending: Deceptive financial characterization under FTC Act Section 5. Roundups merely transfer a user's own existing funds from checking to savings; claiming it 'saved them from that same spending' deceptively conflates asset reallocation with net cost savings or merchant discounts.

**GPT-6.1 Sol — The Spare Change Diaries**  
_Same coffee. Different ending._  
Twenty microcreators document 30 days of rounding up purchases they already make, turning ordinary spending into an honest savings story. Each series starts with a prediction and ends with the actual result—not a guaranteed savings claim. Test creator hooks in week one, then amplify the strongest performers through paid partnership ads. Each concept here is an alternative using the full $80,000 budget.  
Plan: 90 days, $80,000 total: $20,000 for creator partnerships and usage rights; $42,000 for TikTok, Reels, and app-install retargeting; $8,000 for onboarding and premium-conversion experiments; $5,000 for attribution and creative editing; $5,000 contingency. Show real product behavior and avoid encouraging extra purchases.  
KPI: 90-day stretch targets: 100,000 attributable downloads and 5,000 premium subscribers, representing 5% download-to-premium conversion. Target paid CPI at or below $1.25; creator distribution, sharing, and organic discovery must supply roughly 66,400 downloads. Track activated users, not just installs, and shift spend weekly toward creators producing retained users.  
Claims flagged: 4
  - Twenty microcreators document 30 days of rounding up purchases they already make... Each series starts with a prediction and ends with the actual result: Under FTC Endorsement Guides (16 CFR Part 255), user testimonials depicting specific financial savings require clear and conspicuous disclosures of typical results if the creator's savings are atypical, as well as conspicuous disclosure of paid/material connections on all creator content.
  - Student ambassadors run dorm demos and publish weekly school leaderboards: Compensated student ambassadors promoting a commercial financial product on campus and social media must clearly and conspicuously disclose their paid relationship/material connection under FTC Endorsement Guides.
  - Distribute it through financial-aid offices: Distributing a commercial financial app through university financial-aid offices risks deceptively implying official institutional, university, or federal financial-aid endorsement/affiliation in violation of FTC Act Section 5 and applicable Department of Education third-party marketing rules.

**Gemini 3.1 Pro — The Ghost Savings Challenge**  
_Link it, live your life, and see what you saved in 30 days._  
A 30-day challenge where users link their cards, live normally, and reveal their 'Ghost Savings' at the end of the month on TikTok using a custom CapCut template showing their shock at how much they saved.  
Plan: $30k CapCut template creators, $40k Meta/TikTok paid acquisition, $10k cash prize pool for the most viral reveal videos.  
KPI: Achieve a $0.60 CPI through UGC network effects to easily hit 100k DLs. Hit 5% Premium CVR by offering a 'Ghost Multiplier' (accelerated 2x/3x round-ups feature) heavily pushed on Day 30.  
Claims flagged: 4
  - Invisible savings = free money.: Deceptive under Section 5 of the FTC Act. Round-up features transfer the consumer's own deposited funds into a savings bucket; mischaracterizing a debit of the consumer's own money as 'free money' is false and misleading regarding financial terms.
  - refer a friend, both get 1 month Premium free [subsidizes the initial Premium trial, artificially boosting CVR to 5%+ by hooking them on paid features early]: Triggers FTC Negative Option Rule and ROSCA (Restore Online Shoppers' Confidence Act) requirements. If the free month rolls over into a recurring $4.99/month charge, failure to provide clear, conspicuous disclosures of terms and an easy cancellation mechanism prior to collecting billing information constitutes an unlawful deceptive practice.
  - $10k cash prize pool for the most viral reveal videos: Violates FTC Endorsement Guides (16 CFR Part 255) if participants do not clearly and conspicuously disclose their material connection (e.g., #ContestEntry or #PennyContest) in user-generated videos. Running a viral video contest tied to cash prizes also triggers state contest/sweepstakes registration, official rules, and anti-lottery regulations.

**Grok 4.7 — Stitch the Receipt**  
_Your receipt has a secret savings account._  
Seed a 7-second format, not a brand campaign: film the receipt, smash-cut to the round-ups Penny would have kept, stitch or duet to roast or flex. Paid media only amplifies formats the audience already copies, so most installs are earned.  
Plan: TikTok and IG Reels over 90 days. $36k spark and whitelist on winning stitches, $24k to 50 micro-creators ($400–$600) strictly to seed the format, $12k editor pod plus deferred deep links, $8k ASO and Apple Search Ads on branded queries the trend creates. Weekly kill-switch on any creator whose installs do not activate.  
KPI: 100k installs with at least 55% organic or earned; blended CPI at or under $0.80; 40% D1 activation (card link or first manual round-up); 5% premium by day 45 among activated users. Kill criterion: under 8k stitches by day 30, shift remaining media to winners only.  
Claims flagged: 5
  - Your receipt has a secret savings account.: Misleading terminology under banking regulations and FTC Section 5; Penny is an app, not a bank, and receipts do not contain bank deposits or FDIC-insured savings accounts without appropriate disclosures.
  - $24k to 50 micro-creators ($400–$600) strictly to seed the format, stitch or duet to roast or flex: FTC Endorsement Guides violation risk if creators do not clearly and conspicuously disclose paid material connections (e.g., #ad) within organic-looking stitches and duets.
  - DoorDash Demon, Target Trance, 2am Uber: Lanham Act trademark infringement and dilution risk; using registered marks (DoorDash, Target, Uber) in commercial ad creative creates false association, affiliation, or disparagement.

## Brief: `scribe_ai`

**Claude Fable 5.1 — The Founding 30**  
_Ask Dr. Patel in Tulsa why she stopped charting at night._  
Physicians buy from physicians. Recruit 30 independent primary-care docs across 10 states as named 'Founding Clinicians' who get Notewell free for a year in exchange for an honest case study, one 20-minute peer webinar, and one talk at their county/state AFP or medical society meeting. Each Founder gets a personal referral code worth a free month for both parties. The campaign is the Founders themselves — their faces, their specialty, their before/after note times.  
Plan: Founder stipends and comped seats ($30K); 30 local medical-society talks incl. travel, sponsorship fees, and lunch ($28K); 12 peer-led webinars with paid promotion to registrants ($20K); referral engine + landing page per Founder ($12K); PR and physician-podcast guest placements for Founders ($15K); community management + email nurture ($10K); reserve $5K.  
KPI: 30 Founders live by month 2; each Founder drives ≥9 paid referrals (270) plus ~50 from webinar/PR = 300 paid by month 6; referral-sourced CAC ≤$250.  
Claims flagged: 6
  - Hours Back Calculator (visits/day × minutes/note) that spits out a personalized 'evenings reclaimed per year' number / Get your 9 PM back: Unsubstantiated quantifiable time-savings claim under FTC Act Section 5. Calculators promising specific hours or evenings returned require competent and reliable substantiation demonstrating typicality, or clear and conspicuous disclosures of material assumptions and that individual results will vary.
  - Founding Clinicians who get Notewell free for a year in exchange for an honest case study... Founder stipends and comped seats... their before/after note times: Violation of FTC Endorsement Guides if material connections (free $2,400/year subscriptions, stipends, referral compensation) are not clearly and conspicuously disclosed in case studies, talks, and webinars. Furthermore, individual before/after note times represent performance claims that must reflect generally expected results or disclose typical performance.
  - Bring your worst visit. We'll draft the note before you finish your coffee.: Unsubstantiated objective speed and capability claim. Live demonstration handling complex clinical encounters (e.g., medication reconciliation) implies clinical accuracy and completeness under challenging conditions without clinical validation, risking deceptive performance claims under Lanham Act / FTC Act.

## Brief: `sneaker`

**Claude Fable 5.1 — Not a Lecture**  
_Made from ocean plastic. That's the last time we'll mention it._  
Tone as the differentiator. This audience is eco-curious, not eco-evangelist, and is exhausted by guilt-trip sustainability marketing. Loop is the sneaker brand that refuses to preach: we state the recycled-plastic fact once, flatly, then shut up about it and sell a good-looking $139 shoe at a normal price. The restraint itself becomes the shareable joke and the credibility signal — the brand confident enough not to over-claim. Creative is deadpan, short, and quietly funny; price is always in frame.  
Plan: Short-form paid video (TikTok, IG Reels, YouTube Shorts) — 25+ deadpan 6–15s cuts, continuous creative refresh: $110K. 15 comedy/lifestyle/fashion creators briefed on the 'no-lecture' bit, performance-paid (flat + CPA bonus): $45K. Wild postings in NYC/LA/Chicago with single-line deadpan copy: $25K. Google branded/non-brand search: $20K. Production (one 2-day shoot, modular cuts): $25K. Email/SMS CRM: $10K. Contingency: $15K. Total $250K.  
KPI: 8,000 units at blended CAC ≤ $31. Paid video thumbstop rate ≥ 30%, CPA ≤ $33 on cold, ≤ $18 on retargeting. Organic share rate ≥ 2× category benchmark (the joke has to travel). Brand recall lift ≥ 15 pts in a $10K post-campaign survey.  
Claims flagged: 6
  - Estimated stack of a comparable $140 big-brand sneaker, showing the money goes into the shoe, not the swoosh. / Ask Nike for theirs.: Lanham Act § 43(a) and FTC comparative advertising violation. Publicly publishing an 'estimated' cost-stack of a specific named competitor (Nike) without competent and reliable proprietary substantiation constitutes deceptive disparagement and unsubstantiated comparative claims.
  - Pulled from the Gulf of Thailand, March 2025.: FTC Green Guides violation. The brief specifies 'ocean-bound plastic' (terrestrial waste collected near coastlines), but claiming it was 'pulled from the Gulf' deceptively implies it was recovered directly from the marine environment. Additionally, lot-level geographic claims require strict, auditable chain-of-custody substantiation.
  - Your old sneakers are worth $25. Even the ugly ones.: FTC Act § 5 deceptive pricing. Framing a conditional promotional coupon ($25 off a new $139 purchase) as an unconditional cash valuation or currency trade-in ('worth $25') misleads consumers regarding the actual terms and monetary value of the promotion.
